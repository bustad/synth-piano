Short manual

Playing

Click or touch the keys. Drag across them for a glissando; chords work with multitouch. On a computer keyboard the Z–M and Q–U rows play upward from C3 (S D G H J and 2 3 5 6 7 are the black keys).

Sound

Pick a piano or synth sound from the Sound menu. Synth and retro sounds hold for as long as you hold the key. Reverb mix sets how much room you hear, Room size sets how long the tail is (it updates when you let go of the slider), and Volume is the master level.

Changing the pitch

  • Transpose shifts the pitch by semitones (±24). 🎲 Random picks a new random shift between −12 and +12, and Reset returns to 0.
  • Octave shifts by whole octaves (±2) and is never changed by Random or Reset.
  • The keys on screen never move. Only the sound changes, and only for notes you play afterwards.

Sight-singing trainer

  1. Press Select and click the notes you want to practise. They get blue dots (you hear a short preview). Click again to remove one, or press Clear.
  2. Set the Tempo (10–100 BPM; the slider is logarithmic, so the slow end is finely adjustable).
  3. Press Play. On every beat a random selected note lights up green and stays lit until the next beat. The same note never appears twice in a row, and no jump is larger than the Max leap (default 12 semitones, one octave; if no other selected note is within reach, the same note repeats and the status line says so). Press Stop to end.
  4. Play sound lets you hear the note too. Sound delay sets how long after the light it sounds, as a fraction of a beat, so you can sing first and then check yourself. Turn Play sound off to train from sight alone.

Home and scale degrees

  • Treat middle C (the key marked Do) as home: 1, or Do. Keep doing this whatever Transpose and Octave are set to. Those settings change the pitch you hear, not which key is home, so you always sing each lit key as its degree relative to middle C.
  • Sing the name of every note as it lights up, either as a scale degree (1, 2, 3, ♭3, 4, ♯4 …) or in solfege (Do, Re, Mi, Me …). Naming a note ties its sound to its job in the key.
  • Use the Solfege ladder to find the name of any of the 12 chromatic tones. Hold a row to hear the pitch, and tick “Show the lit note on the ladder” when you want to check your answer.

Drone

Press Drone to hold a steady Do (the note C) while you sing. Choose its octave, pick any sustaining sound and set its level. The drone follows Transpose, so it always stays on your Do, but it ignores the Octave setting, which only moves the keys. Singing against a drone makes it much easier to hear whether you are in tune with home. A quiet, pure sound such as Organ, Glass pad or 8-bit triangle works well.

Sound editor

Open the Sound editor under the keyboard to change the sound chosen in the Sound menu. Each sound has up to 8 oscillators (waveform, pitch multiple, detune in cents, level and stereo pan), an amplitude envelope (attack, decay, sustain, release), a filter envelope (start, attack, peak, decay, sustain, release) and a stereo chorus. Changes apply to the next note you play. You can rename a sound, save a copy as a new sound, delete it, or reset it to the built-in version, and your edits are remembered with your other settings.

Saved settings

Your settings (sound, reverb, volume, transpose, octave, selected notes, tempo, delay, max leap and the drone options) are remembered automatically in this browser and come back the next time you open the page. The drone and the trainer always start switched off. Export settings at the bottom saves everything (settings, edited sounds and your own sounds) to a file you can keep as a backup or open on another computer with Import settings. Reset all settings clears the memory and restores the defaults.

Tips

Start with two or three notes at a slow tempo and a long delay, then add notes and speed up. Use Random transpose to practise relative pitch: the shapes stay the same but the notes you hear change. Wobble bass sounds best with Octave set to −1 or −2. The pure-sine sounds (Mellow, Music box, Glass pad, Organ, 8-bit triangle) are the easiest to match with your voice.
